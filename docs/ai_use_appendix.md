# AI-use appendix

**Where AI helped.** AI-assisted discussion helped compare finance workflow problems, explore PDF/OCR implementation options, design the prototype, inspect failure modes, and organize the test plan. The runtime uses local PDF text extraction and OCR. An optional language-model path proposes candidate fields for unfamiliar layouts, with source-text checks; it never calculates a payable decision. Representative prompts used during development included: “from where are u planning to take the boiler plate and which ocr tool?” and “our project should be unique ... figure out what else we can develop.”

**What was rejected or corrected.** A candidate invoice repository was rejected after source inspection because active matching depended on filename fragments and normalized invoice and purchase-order records were not populated reliably. A one-call model approval design was also rejected because source links, arithmetic, cumulative PO balances, and changing evidence need inspectable state and exact calculations. Graph storage, autonomous agents, and a vector index were considered unnecessary for the bounded checks in this prototype.

**What came from the candidate.** The candidate chose the problem of invoice decisions changing when supporting evidence changes later. The key design choice was to separate probabilistic document perception from deterministic financial rules, keep every accepted value tied to source evidence, and require explicit human-controlled replay when a receipt correction or credit changes history. The project uses synthetic data, keeps payment and tax accountability with a qualified human, and treats the editable PO as a scenario rather than proof of an authorized amendment.

**One thing to defend without AI.** The strongest example is the PO-versus-receipt distinction. When PO-1002 is revised from 200 to 250 units, 225 billed units fit within the order but exceed the original 200-unit receipt, so the invoice moves from rejection to review rather than approval. Only after a source-backed receipt amendment confirms 250 units can the decision move to approval. This explains why cumulative quantity and gross are recalculated after credits or receipt corrections and why a shortfall requires human review.
